# Human-Computer Interaction

The empirical and design discipline at the boundary between people and systems: how to study users rigorously, how to design and prototype interfaces, how to evaluate them with valid methods and statistics, and how the field is being reshaped by AI-mediated interaction. For a CS researcher, the core skill is treating interaction as an object of *scientific* study — hypotheses, controlled experiments, and honest measurement of human behavior. Adjacent subfields — CSCW/social computing and information visualization — are sampled via the CHI proceedings here rather than treated as full units.

## Learning objectives

- Apply human-factors foundations: perception, attention, memory, motor control, and models like Fitts's law and GOMS to predict interaction cost.
- Run the design process — needfinding, ideation, prototyping at varying fidelity, iteration — and justify design decisions against user needs.
- Design and analyze rigorous evaluations: controlled experiments, within/between-subjects designs, appropriate statistics, and qualitative methods (think-aloud, interviews, thematic analysis).
- Reason about usability, accessibility, and inclusive design as first-class requirements, not afterthoughts.
- Critically assess human-AI interaction: mental models, trust and calibration, explanation, and the failure modes of AI-mediated interfaces.

## Prerequisites

- [Research Methods](../core/research-methods.md) — experimental design, statistics, and research ethics.
- No deep systems prerequisites; programming for prototyping is helpful.

## Primary resources

- **Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*** — the conceptual foundations (affordances, mappings, feedback).
- **Lazar, Feng & Hochheiser, *Research Methods in Human-Computer Interaction*** — the methods spine.
- **Shneiderman et al., *Designing the User Interface*** — comprehensive reference.
- **UW CSE 440 / Stanford CS147 / Berkeley CS160** — HCI design-studio courses (free materials).
- **The ACM CHI proceedings** — the field's primary research venue; read for method and rigor.

## Seminal papers

- Fitts (1954), "The Information Capacity of the Human Motor System in Controlling the Amplitude of Movement."
- Card, Moran & Newell (1983), *The Psychology of Human-Computer Interaction* — GOMS, the model-human-processor.
- Nielsen & Molich (1990), "Heuristic Evaluation of User Interfaces."
- Hutchins, Hollan & Norman (1985), "Direct Manipulation Interfaces."
- Weiser (1991), "The Computer for the 21st Century" — ubiquitous computing.
- Amershi et al. (2019), "Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction."
- Bansal et al. (2021), "Does the Whole Exceed Its Parts? The Effect of AI Explanations on Complementary Team Performance."
- Horvitz (1999), "Principles of Mixed-Initiative User Interfaces."

## Assignments

- **Problem sets:** weekly — one experiment-design critique, one statistics/analysis problem, one Fitts/GOMS predictive-model calculation.
- **Design project:** run a full design cycle on a real problem — needfinding → low- and high-fidelity prototypes → a usability evaluation — and report what each iteration changed and why.
- **Empirical study:** design and run a small controlled experiment comparing two interfaces or interaction techniques, with a pre-registered hypothesis, appropriate statistics, and an honest threats-to-validity section.
- **Paper critique:** deep-read the Human-AI Interaction guidelines or the AI-explanations paper via `/paper` — interrogate the methods and whether the conclusions follow.

## AI study loop

- `/study human-computer-interaction` for concept passes; design the study before reading the method.
- `/quiz human-computer-interaction` weekly — experimental design and statistics are the highest-yield drills.
- `/oral-exam human-computer-interaction`; expect "what's your hypothesis, your design, and your threats to validity?"

## Mastery checklist

- [ ] Can apply Fitts's law / GOMS to predict interaction performance.
- [ ] Can run a needfinding-to-prototype design cycle and justify decisions from evidence.
- [ ] Can design a valid controlled experiment and choose the correct statistical test.
- [ ] Can conduct and analyze a qualitative study (think-aloud, thematic analysis).
- [ ] Can evaluate accessibility and articulate inclusive-design requirements.
- [ ] Can connect the topic to current research (human-AI teaming, trust calibration, explainability, mixed-initiative and agentic interfaces).
